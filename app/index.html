Texas tax-foreclosure screening playbook

A practical research sequence for Chapter 34 sales and resales. County procedures and individual court orders control, so treat every summary below as a prompt to verify—not as the sale terms.
Scope matters. This app tracks court-ordered sales and resales of real property under Texas Tax Code Chapter 34. It is not a marketplace for property-tax loan interests. Texas separately regulates licensed property-tax lenders and certain transfers of tax liens under Tax Code §32.06; those transactions are outside this workflow.

1 · Locate the controlling notice

  • Chapter 34 sales are tied to a judgment and order of sale. Confirm the cause number, legal description, account number, minimum bid, sale status, officer or online host, designated place/method, and exact payment deadline.
  • The ordinary statutory sale window is the first Tuesday of the month between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. If that first Tuesday is January 1 or July 4, the statute moves the sale to the first Wednesday. A sale may also be conducted online when the statutory requirements are met.
  • A county may adopt bidder-registration requirements under §34.011. Do not assume registration is required—or that another county's form works—until you verify the local rule and deadline.

2 · Distinguish auction property from resale inventory

  • If no sufficient bid is received, property may be bid off to a taxing unit. Later disposition can occur through statutory public or private resale procedures, depending on the circumstances and required consents.
  • Ask the named taxing unit or counsel who accepts offers, what minimum is authorized, which entities must consent, what deed will be delivered, and whether the property remains subject to redemption.
  • Never describe a resale as a normal warranty-deed closing unless the actual documents support that conclusion. Current statutory notices emphasize “as is, where is” treatment and the absence of warranties.

3 · Classify redemption before pricing the deal

Federal interests require separate analysis. In certain judicial or nonjudicial foreclosure settings, the United States may have a 120-day redemption period—or a longer state-law period—and defective notice or joinder can affect whether a federal lien is discharged. Escalate any federal-lien finding to qualified counsel.

4 · Complete parcel, title, and physical diligence

  • Identity: reconcile the notice, judgment, CAD record, map, acreage, situs, legal description, and account number. Similar names and tiny remnant tracts are common screening traps.
  • Value: do not use CAD value as market value. Build an exit estimate from relevant sales, realistic market time, access, utilities, flood/buildability, condition, and title marketability.
  • Money: verify minimum bid, post-judgment/current taxes, assessments, municipal charges, POA claims, closing/title work, insurance, possession, cleanup, repairs, holding time, and resale costs.
  • Rights and restrictions: review the court file and land records for parties, easements, access, restrictions, mineral issues, federal interests, and other matters that may survive or impair marketable title.
  • Site feasibility: confirm legal and physical access, flood zone/floodway, water and wastewater options, subdivision/building rules, environmental concerns, occupancy, and safe lawful inspection limits.

5 · Use the dashboard as a gate, not a verdict

  • Enter a conservative verified exit value and every expected non-bid cost. The dashboard calculates max bid under three simultaneous caps: maximum all-in percentage, minimum profit, and minimum ROI.
  • A GO label also requires at least 75% of the core diligence fields and no high/critical risk flag. A HOLD means the economics may be interesting but a risk or diligence gate is open. PASS means entered economics miss the buy box after the diligence gate is met.
  • The score is a transparent sorting aid. It is not an appraisal, title opinion, legal conclusion, probability of redemption, or substitute for professional review.

6 · Call script

“I'm researching the property identified by account number and cause number on the current tax-sale material. Could you confirm: (1) whether it is still active and the controlling notice/order; (2) the sale method, registration, and payment requirements; (3) the minimum bid and any later taxes or charges I should verify; and (4) the correct contact for court-file, resale, and redemption questions?”

7 · Glossary

  • Minimum bid — the statutory or court-order opening amount for the sale; verify the current figure.
  • Bid off / struck off — property acquired for a taxing unit after no sufficient bid, potentially followed by a statutory resale.
  • Purchaser's deed / taxing unit's deed — the recorded deed that starts the Chapter 34 redemption period described above.
  • Redemption — the former owner's or other eligible party's statutory right to recover property by paying the required amounts within the applicable period.
  • Title curative work — legal and record work needed to establish insurable/marketable title; the correct path depends on the file and title requirements.
